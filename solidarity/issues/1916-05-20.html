In 1914 the United Garment Workers of America held its 18th annual convention in Nashville, Tenn. At the opening of the convention, certain delegates, although elected by their respective locals, were not seated about 100 in all. A number of seated delegates rebelled against such action and decided among themselves that either these 100 protested delegates should be seated, or they themselves would buck the convention. President Rickert, knowing beforehand, that the eastern and some of the western delegates were opposed to his presidency, and not caring to lose his high-salaried position he was afraid if all delegates were seated he (Rickert) would lose out, and so the undesirable ones were excluded. Rickert knew that new ideas spelled doom for him, and at any cost, he would rule the convention. Little did Rickert and his followers care, when this body of men, sooner than see their fellow workers denied all rights they were justly entitled to, left the hall. No persuasion or reason would Rickert & Co. listen to. The non-seated delegates would not be recognized, and so all delegates, desirable and otherwise, decided to break away from the U. G. W. of A They decided to se cede and form an independent organization. How those boys fought, and how earnest they were in building up a new, a democratic organization. They decided to elect their own officers, and work in behalf of all the tailors. They were going to emancipate the workers.
