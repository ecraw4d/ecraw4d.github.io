The General Strike in the above-mentioned States, is the workers' reply to Military autocracy, and State ambserviency to the rule of the Lumber Barons, as personified by beardless lleutenants, pompous colonela, astnine sheriffs, "Town Clowns," swollen purple with importance, and State Execu tives who have cast aside all scruples, and disregarded every prestige of human liberty in their mad anxiety to serve Mammon, and to show that as sgainst human versus property rights thef are first, last and all the time devotees at the Shrine of the Almighty Dollar. Like France and England. This General Strike in the Northwest will go down in history as the most Imposing display of working-class Solidarity as yet exhibited by the workers of America, for let ft be known that this General Strike is to this country what the General Strike of the French workers, which released Durand from the clutches of the French capitalists, was to France, what the great strike of the British workers-which freed from dungeons dark Tom Mann and Jim Larkin-was to Great Britain, for this strike, my friends, is a Class-War Strike, to release not one worker, or two workers of prominence from the clutches of Capitalism, but to release hundreds of prisoners, obscure and unknown, the majority of them, from the "Bull-pens," Stockades and jails of the Northwest, where they are held by Military and State authorities, in the greater number of cases without any charges being preferred against them, without warrant and due process of law, and incommunicado. Demoerasy vs. Autocracy, This General Dirike is fundamentally a strike to secure and maintain certain rights, that the workers must possess, in order to survive as freemen. It is a counterattack against the onslaughts of despotism, it is Solidarity of the workers as against the Iron Heel of the Lamber-trust. The Strike, in addition to demanding the release of all Class-war prisoners, is also called to preserve to the workers, the right to organize, the right to maintain their halls, to transact their legitimate and proper business in con nection, the right to be secure in their persons and property, against despotic Invasionis by city, state or military authorities. This Strike brings the issue squarely before the people, the issue is Democracy or Autocracy, if the blood of the workers is still red, if it has not turned to water in their veins, if their courage is still undiminished in spite of jalls, murder and oppression. if they are not pallid with fear, and weak with cowardice, we know the fight can only end one way, victory for the workers, release of all Class-war pris oners, the rights they are striking for will be theirs. But, as in every movement of this kind, we also had a few strikebreakers. Some of them came to our side, but there were a few who didn't. Monday morning the shipowners went to the shipping commissioners crying that we, the mem bers of the L. W. W., want to do this and that with them. The authorities, who are always in accord with the side of capital, sent soldiers to the plers, not allowing anyone to pass through the gates. They also refused to per mit us to land on ships or launches hired by us. Seeing that this was to break the movement, we decided to call a general strike of all the workers in Tampico. We began an agitation among all the workers showing that an injury to one is an injury to all, and when the workers realized it they were ready to come out. When the authorities saw this revolt they called the secretary of the M. T. W. to have an interview with them, but the secretary told them that we had nothing to do with them as we were dealing with the companies and would deal with nobody else; also it was against our principles. After waiting for the owners to come to us, instead they went to the authorities crying for help. Then we called all the workers out, stopping all traffic. For it is an historic fact that any form of oppressiog breeds among the oppressed a spirit of revolt, which results ultimately in revolution. The pages of history are red with the blood of unknown thou sands of slaves who have striven unsuccessfully to throw off the shackles. Many have suffered torture, imprisonment and death in the struggle. Innumerable rebellions have arisen and been sup pressed in a welter of blood, for the ruling class have always proven themselves to be ruthless in dealing with rebellious slaves. But in spite of past defeats, the class-war still goes on. It can neither be stopped, postponed, or averted, much though the cap italists and their henchmen may prate of the identity of interests between capital and labor, and then give the lie to their words by jailing members of the working-class for preaching enlightenment to the slaves, The oldest form of slavery is that known as chattel-slavery. The slave was the absolute property of the master, bought and sold on the market, to work wherever and under any conditions the master desired. Toiling and sweating for terribly long hours and re ceiving only enough food to keep them alive, while the ruling class rioted in luxury, debauchery, and idleness. With the development of the methods of production, the slave system grew and expanded until it reached its culminating point in the great Roman Empire, which was hailed as the highest form of civilization possible for the human race to achieve. A comparison of the economic position of the two classes will show how much that civilization was really worth. The aristocracy, on the one hand, enjoyed great wealth, all of the comforts which it was pos sible for the ingenuity of the slaves to conceive. Knowledge, art, and all the sciences flourished and prospered, while every form of debauchery gave proof of the moral deterioration to which an idle class is always prone. But for the slaves there existed only misery and degradation, Liable to be separated from wife and children at the mere whim of his master, his daughters the prey of the patrician debauchee, revolt against such conditions was inevitable. Australia Finds to Her Sorrow That the One Big Union Idea Is Indestructible At the present moment all sorts of stories are being spread about the 1. W. W. They are red-handed amarchists one day, mollycoddles the next; on the following day, bums; and the day after that they are in the pay of the German Government. How can anyone be a bûm and still darw big sums from the Kaiser? We do not know; we shall leave that question to Mountain Esau Landis, the Judge of Miscarriage of Justice. The unfortunate part about the 1 W. W. is that they are not respectable. They go out on the job and wear greasy overalls; go out in the harvest fields and sleep on bundles of hay; they ride freight trains, whereas if they were respectable they would go and address big mass meetings in the auditoriums, theaters, etc., and pass-str-r-rong r-r-resolutions. If you address mass meetings Mistawh Clabaugh of the secret service just smiles; he knows that you do not throw any monkey wrenches into the industrial machine. But if you go on the job, and in a most unceremonious manner refuse to pass a strong resolution, but just get to business and do something, then for Christ's sake how can you be respectable? How Dare the Workers! What do the workers do, anyway? All that the I. W. W. does is to do the dirty work of society. Whilst the intellectual is in the drawing room discussing why poets go hungry, or why Gompers is grumpy, the I. W. W. is down in the mines bringing up the ore that goes to make the steel rails that go to transport the things we need. Some go out in the lumber camps and hew down the mighty trees, so that the intellectual can loll back in the nice easy chair. Some go out in the harvest fields, so that the intellectual might eat. All through our life the poor worker is doing things, whilst the intellectual is talking. As in peace times, so in war times. Whilst the intellectuals were passing strong resolutions, the I. W. W. were doing things. They did not pass strong resolutions, they just issued their ultimatum and told the boss where to get off. When the life of the workers was attacked they did not talk and merely make a buzzing sound in the ears of the masters, but they went for his very soul, that is, his bank roll. They knew where he centered all his thoughts; they knew the quickening impulse of his whole existence and they went for it. Like a mad tiger, the capitalist class were aroused and they showed their claws. How dare the workers seek to stop our onward march? When we say to the workers fight, they must fight, and to those who dare say nay, then we shall prove to them what we are made of. The Hard Fight in Australia. There is one part of the world where the I. W. W. flourishes, and where the fight has been a hard one; that is in Australia. You can talk of Belgium and you can shed bitter tears of feeling against Germany, because of the contrast between the two nations. But what shall we say of the Government of Australia, a government that has thousands of soldiers, sailors, policemen and officers to back it up? A government that has thousands of offices and big buildings 'so large, is its work. A government that can spend money as fast as it cares to, that can call out any time all the forces within its borders. Picture on the other hand a small group of workers, with a small office in a back street, just managing to live. The only assistance they get is from the poor underpaid worker; they go out addressing outdoor and indoor meetings. They go to the factory, the mines and the docks to organize the workers, so that the workers can get more of the good things of life. They work night and day, so that the workers may see the light and find their way out of the darkness of ignorance. So successful is their propaganda, that when the government seeks to send the workers to the trenches, the I. W. W. cried halt, and the government had to stop. The warships could not get coal, the people had to do without bread and other necessaries, because the industrial machine had been stopped. Like Wild Tigers for Prey. The kind, good Government, by the people, for the people, to do the people, was aroused, and so they hunted like mad tigers for prey, and they fastened their claws upon the I. W. W. As a result they sent many members of the I. W. W. to prison for terms ranging from five to fifteen years. It meant that most of these men would not see their wives and children again for years; it meant the sacrifice of all that was worth while, but they did not whine. They stood defiant in the dock, and they told the judge that if they ever came out they would take up the battle where they left off. Their wives stood in court the day they were tried; they were human, and they shed tears because they were parting from their loved ones. But these women did not worry about the material things of life; they wanted their husbands back in the fight. The boys who were left to carry on the organization did not sit idly back and allow these sentences to stay their work; they went direct from the court house to the public square and held a protest meeting and collected $55. You cannot argue with that spirit; that is the kind of spirit that of spirit that made ther of Russia to kingdopossible. You cannot do with that spirit what you think fit; you MAY try to crush it.
