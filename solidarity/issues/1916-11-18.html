The election returns show that Wilson received the overwhelming support of the South and West of the rural and less developed industrial sections of the country. Whether this was due to the great opposition in those quarters to war by the United States, or to the unprecedented prosperity of the farmers, is immaterial. At any rate, it was to be expected; and the only doubt hinged on the attitude of the industrial East. Would there be a clean sweep for Hughes, here? There wasn't, and Wilson won the election. What is there in all this for the working class? Certainly not the hope that big capitalism will receive any check in its development. Anyone who hugs the delusion that Wall Street will cease to dominate the economic life of the United States, or that the big corporations will not wax fatter during Wilson's second term, are due for an awakening. Nothing is surer than the opposite. All that remains for the workers, then, is to organize on their side as strongly as the big capitalists are organized on theirs. Remember, that Ludlow, and Bayonne, and Mesaba and Everett, occurred in Wilson's adimnistration; as similar outrageous actions against workers had occurred in previous Republican administrations. The class war is still on, and will become more bitter and relentless each month and year that is to come. Organization, on an ever bigger scale, by the workers, is the only answer. The I. W. W. is still here, though innumerable efforts have been made to kill it the past four years, as previously. You workers would do well to investigate its program right now. It offers the only effective means of opposition to big capitalism. It is organized, to protect the workers against the aggressions of their masters now and up to the point where the workers shall be sufficiently organized to take over the reins of industry and society for themselves, thus eliminating the masters.
