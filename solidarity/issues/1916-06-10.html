Mr. Gompers, political nominee and lobbyist, hurled a boomerang at his pet theory when he threw the above, Unionism In Chain Factories Elsewhere in this issue will be found a communication regarding the necessity of organising all the chain factories of corporations in order to secure beneficial results. This communica tion is timely. There is no doubt that complete organization is the ideal toward which labor must strive. It is good for labor to keep this ideal in mind. But this ideal may be insisted on too strongly, and instead of proving an incentive, becomes a discouragement to organization of any kind. This is to be avoided, as it is more beneficial to labor to have incomplete organization and revolts than to be despirited and submissive. Even the presence of disorganized discontent is to be encouraged, for the manifestations of its existence often prompts. improvement in wages and conditions. Besides it does not always follow that, because of the existence of a chain of factories, no change can be made in any one factory in that chain. That ali depends on the peculiar use to which that factory may he put, and the specific reasons for its location in certain territories. A certain factory in a chain of factories may be a specialized fadly, devoted to the manufacture of certain specialties not made in other factories. To strike in that factory is to stop the production of those specialties. Again a factory may be located near the field of distribution, or market. This is done to save freight charges and to reduce the cost of production. To strike in that factory is to injure the company's business in that territory.
