WORKERS VERSUS MASTERS Prosecution at it With a Vengeance- Boys in Jail Singing Songs By Charles Ashleigh. (Special to "Solidarity.") It is no ordinary trial which is taking place in Seattle, Wash., at the present moment when Thomas H. Tracy is on trial for the murder of deputy Jefferson Beard. This is a law case in which not an individual, but an or ganization, is on trial. The expenditure of thousands of dollars for publicity through the conventional mediums could not have obtained as much interest of a widespread nature as has this momentous trial. Hundreds of people have heard the principles and tactics of the L. W. W. expatiated upon in the course of the long and bitterly contested legal battle. The papers of Seattle have commented upon the fact that Tracy's name. and personal doings hardly ever come up in the course of the case. It is true Tracy himself is not being tried! Tracy is merely the personification, the momentarily adopted symbol of the revolutionary labor movement. The crucial question of the case is the matter of the First Shot. From where it came, and who fire those are the vital problems upon the solu tion of which depend the l or lifelong imprisonment of 74 men. So far, the testimony offered and all testimony so far has been that of the State's witnesses-has been of a confused and self-contradictory nature. The frst shot, it has been alleged by the witness-stand pets of the prosecution. has come from 'midships, from the sta from the forward part of the ship, from the cabin, from the main deck and from the hurricane deck. Out of this mass of conflicting testimony only one thing emerges: that is, that the witnesses of the prosecution have been at least sufficiently drilled to stick! like leeches to the blind and groundless assertion that the first shot came from the ship. This, of course, was to be expected. Had they not been willing to so testify, they would not have been asked to take the stand for the state. The Mayor of Everett, D. D. Merrill, convicted by the sworn statement of Fellow, Worker Louis Skaroff as an inhuman brute of the vilest nature, shiv ered in the stand under the searing cross-examination of our attorneys, Fred Moore and George Vanderveer. He denied in toto the allegations of brutalty. When asked whether or not he had beaten up Skaroff and had had the boy's fingers placed, one at a time, under the castors of a bed upon whigh three men were sitting, he replied in a weak and nervous negative. Then, dramatically, at the crucial mement, Skaroff was brought into court and the degenerate torturer was confronted with his hapless victim. "Do you know this boy?" asked Vanderveer in thunderous tones. The Mayor this eminently respectable pillar of Everett society, the incarnation and official representative of the "Everett spirit," that thing that stinks to the high heavens-blanched as white as the livers of the Commercial Club deputies when he saw the frail boy upon whom he had helped to wreak his beastly intentions. Thick and obstructed was his utterance when he mplied unintelligibly to the question. One of the most dramatic moments ever witnessed in a court of law was this. And the crowded courtroom shuddered with horror that in a so-called sane world such things could be. Never has the relentless brutality of the master class been made so apparent as in this trial. Especially with regard to the atrocities of Beverly Park, on October 30th, has the public been awakened and dowered suddenly with a vision of the lengths to which the respectable lick-spittles of capitalism will go in their efforts to crush the onward surge of the workers. One great deputy, named Hawes, a man of 6 foot 2 inches in height, and weighing 260 pounds, said that the reason he had chased a kidnapped I. W. W. who endeavored to escape into the woods, was "because he was a big baby." As. these words fell from his thick lips, the doors of the courtroom opened and there entered two of the Beverly Park victims. Slim boys, they were young and unformed and of small stature. Rebels to the core, with the mighty hearts of those who will do all for the movement in which they be lieve, but with bodies sadly out of proportion to the great spirit beating within them. These, Vanderveer stood side by side with the monster Commercial Club member, commanding him to rise. "Now," said Vanderveer, "are these the big cowards and babies of whom you spoke?" The giant, a hulking compound of senseless boss-worship, glowered powerlessly at his erstwhile prey.
