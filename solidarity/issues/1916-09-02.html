If we must have conscription, let us have a conscription of wealth for the common good. Victor Berger favors "a democratic organization of the mil itary system." First thing Vic knows he will be favoring the alcoholization of prohibition-with the aid of the brew that made Milwaukee famous, too. In Frisco the capitalists are advised to end strikes by filling ambulances with strikers. Let the workers retaliate by filling the unions with members and the shops with job control. SOLIDARITY, CLEVELAND, OHIO social tendencies. It is even now said that Socialism will see a great revival after the war, because of the effects of the latter. And so with the I. W. W. It is proclaimed "dead," "a tradition," "impossible," "against prevailing sympathies," and "poltical-aspirations," etc., etc. And yet it grows here, there, and over yonder, somehow and somewhat. It presents the picture of a training ground, where independent groups of soldiers at different points perform successful evolutions, prior to massing for one big mobilization for determined warfare. In other words, the 1. W. W. is repeating history, labor history, all history. It is like all historic beginnings spontaneous, sporadic in a word, formative. It has still to grow and become as triumphant as social tendencies demand and historical analogy warrants. Revolution I had spent a languid summer vacation in a small California township, once a part of old Mexico. A short distance from my cabin were the remains of an old Spanish church, which proclaimed the regular monastic architecture of a past period. There were thick mould encrusted walls, sparse sleeping-cells, but, for me, a congenial outdoor library were the arched cloisters, where the monks, I supposed, were wont to parade, morning and evening to their prayers. The colonade was not as long as some of the same period, but consisted of two sweeping arches supported by a square central column. By accident or design an enormous oak, gnarled, and with a multitude of spreading branches had grown alongside the pillar. Some trees would have branched curvingly around the arches, moulding themselves to the exigency of the situation, but not so this oak, for the hard flooring had been cracked by the slow but forceful expansion of its roots, and even the pillar had been pierced by a branch, imbedded deep in the mortar and causing a yawning fissure. So deep gaped this crack that I wondered the rotten colonade did not crash down about me. And I knew that it was a tree after my own heart and called it "my tree."
