(Special to Solidarity) Seattle, Wash., October, 1916. The women house workers of Seattle are about to rebel. The employers have served notice to the officials of the City Free Employment office, that they would organize and cut the wages to $1.50 for eight hours work. The women house workers are up in arms and threaten a strike, as soon at the bosses make a move. The editor of The Union Record (A. F. of L.) spoke to the women swhile ago, and advised them to form a union and join the A. F. of L Asked wheer the A. F. of L. would back up the women financially and help them organize, by printing leaflets, etc., he said that he could do nothing but give them a free hall in the Labor Temple for the first meeting. A few women, former members of the Garment Workers' Union, told him point blank, that the women would have nothing to do with the A. F. of L.., inasmuch as the Waitress' Union and the Housemaids' Union refused to take in the women house workers or help them to organize. A-hint was made by yours truly, after the A. F. of L. man left, to organize into the I. W. W. But this was also rejected; the daily newspapers have slandered the I. W. W. for years and published false, misleading statements. It was decided to form an independent union (not a yellow me, though) and strike the blow at the right time. In the meantime, sabstage (organized) is being used in a mild form, although most of the workers don't know the word sabotage. Fellow workers can rest assured that the I. W. W. will be the laughing heir to the latest move of the Employers' Association.
