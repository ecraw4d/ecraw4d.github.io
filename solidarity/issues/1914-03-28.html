A Proposed Easter Parade The German Social Democracy The German Social Democratic Party claims to be the labo party, and to represent the proletariat; but it was not founded o inspired by the proletariat, nor are its leaders today working mep. The names Lassalle, Marx, Engels, Liebknecht, Kautsky, Bernstein and Lebedour, all members of the middle class, intellectuals and lawyers, sufficiently prove that labor has nothing to do with the leadership of the party. Bebel started life as a wage-worker, but was an employer of labor and a small capitalist at the time when he became a party leader in the Reichstag, and remained a well-to-do man till his death. And Bebel is the typical German Socialist! The Marxian doctrines first found full expression in the Erfurt Convention of 1891. These doctrines are still nominally retained by the party to this day in the preamble of its platform. This preamble discusses "the ever widening gulf" between the bourgeoisie and the proletariat, and ranges the Social Democratic party on the side of the latter. As a matter of fact the actual tactics and methods of the party representatives in the Reichstag are reformist and "progressive." The activities of the parliamentary group are such as to benefit the middle class and the upper working class, the aristocrats of labor. The middle class, therefore, under the cover of this preamble denying its existence, goes forward towards the completion of the bourgeois revolution and towards gradual disarmament and other measures for the benefit of the tax-payers. This middle class is destined to become the ruling class, the office-holding bureaucracy of State Socialism.
