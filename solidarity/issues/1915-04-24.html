Weekly, One Dollar a Year RANGEL AND CLINE They Have Gone to Prison for Life in the Cause of Labor. No member of the Rangel-Cline group has had to give his life at the end of a rope, as was for a long time feared would be the case, but the efforts put forth in their behalf have not availed to save any of the fourteen men from prison. J. M. Rangel and Charles Cline have been the last to be sentenced. They were sentenced for life. In their first trials the jury in each case disagreed. Young Rosas, a mere boy, who was acquitted and at once rearrested upon another charge, has been held for months in prison. During the past few months the Socialist Local in San Antonio has been very active in behalf of these persecuted comrades, the conduct of the defense having been practically handed over to a committee of Local San Antonio, because of the great difficulty experienced by the Los Angeles Committee in dealing with cases at such a distance. Of Comrade Cline the secretary of the San Antonio Committee writes: "We have investigated his record in the labor movement and it is clean. Because of his activity in the cause of the disinherited he has incurred the ill-will of the masters and must suffer the consequerces, unless the comrades and fellow workers stand by him as loyally as he has stood by them in the past. We who have been present at the "trials" know that Cline has suffered a greater injustice than any other member of the band. His enemies even resorted to the old "criminal record" fake. Just before he was to take the stand his counsel learned of the presence of a man claiming that he knew Cline as an inmate of a Missouri penitentiary, sentenced for murder. It was too late to secure the evidence disproving the story and Cline was prevented taking the stand in his own behalf that it might not reach the jury. The result was a life sentence for our helpless comrade and we see no hope for him except in wide, whitehot publicity." The secretary also writes: "The men were ill-clad and hungry." As indicating the utter hopelessness of getting a fair consideration of these cases in Texas, a comrade wrote the Los Angeles Committee under date of March 17th, enclosing a circular which he said had been widely distributed in and around Sweetwater, Texas. It runs, in large black type: "Don't Foret! Dimmit County Smugglers Moving Picture Film. Queen Picture Show Tonight. 20 Smugglers, 3 killed, 3 escaped, 14 Captured. This Happened September 14, 1913." Then in smaller type is detailed a sensational account of cases from the viewpoint of the prosecution, concluding: "This show is taxing the capacity of show houses All Over the U. S. First show begins 7:30, etc." This public appeal to prejudice through the movies while the cases of Rangel, Cline and Rosas were still pending. A brazen, brutal blow by the strong to the helpless. And nobody in Texas save the little groups of Socialists and the scattered radicals to say one word in protest against so manifest a breach of justice and fair play. Lovers of justice Socialists, Industrialists, Anarchists outside of Texas, should continue their efforts to free these comrades. The San Antonio Committee, of which Mrs. Vera Mayfield, 709 North Brazos street, is secretary, is-soliciting funds for an appeal in Cline's case, at this time.
