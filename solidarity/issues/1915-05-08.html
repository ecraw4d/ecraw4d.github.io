Hay also owns land in Mexico, where his peons bring him a lucrative income. This may also account for his enthusiastic support of such an outfit as the Industrial Inn. The other members of the board of trustees consist of hotch-potch of lawyers, judges, real estate sharks, preachers, doctors and parasites in general. Let all workers walk wide of this Industrial Inn, as it holds out no hope for the workers to better their conditions. It is the entering wedge of a concerted effort to bring about a general reduction of wages in this neck of the woods, and if it has any measure of success will be extended on a larger scale. How to offset this menace will be one of the problems of harvest organization in the Washington harvest fields this year. Certain it is, that those who indulge in the time-honored custom of sitting "neath the bough" in the jungle waiting for John Farmer to come and take them out, will be sadly disappointed, because this year the slaves will be as thick as "leaves in Wallambrosa," and it's the Industrial Inn neople who know it, too. They are going to make strenuous efforts to steer the slaves to John Farmer's harvest fields via the Industrial Inn route. I believe we shall, have to get some of our men in that institution, and certainly the Sab Cat will be a very useful accessory. Last year the farmers and the threshing machine owners were badly hit by smut; large numbers of machines went up in smoke, and thousands of acres were burned and destroyed. These disasters were by some blamed upon the I. W. W. Others said the devil was responsible; while some thought that God was angry with them. It is immaterial to us which version is correct; all we can say is this: If working conditions in the harvest fields are not improved, the chances are that the devil may be more devilish, God more angry, or the 1. W. W. more active. Let us hope the latter. Let us have for our motto this coming harvest, the maxim: "A rebel on the job means a scissorbill off the job."
