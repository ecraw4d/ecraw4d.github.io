The iron heel of capitalism is unescapable, so long as working elasa disunity exists. There can be no iron heel where the class solidarity of labor is present. Small families mean a decreasing labor supply with increased labor demand and wages. It is large families that mean cheap living, because they mean cheap wages. Says the same disciple of the illustrious Marxian further: "Poverty has its roots in exploitation and not in large families." That is true; but it is also true that large families help to accentuate both the exploitation and the poverty. The workingmen with large families cannot fortify themselves for the struggle against exploitation and want as well as the workingmen with small families can. We are also told that "With developing capitalism the workers' condition cannot help but get worse, and birth control will not stave it off. The only remedy that will be of benefit to the workers is to abolish capitalist production and appropriation, and institute social production and appropriation instead. This criticism would have some point, if it could be shown that birth control is intended to either stave off the evils of capitalism or to remedy that system all alone. If we understand birth control aright, it simply aims to contribute some of the forces and aid in the creation of some of the conditions that will make the abolition of capitalism more possible. But, when one stops to consider the wide-spread resort to birth control now going on, as we know, for practical, social purposes, and dictated by the same wage system so learned referred to by our Marxian friend, one wonders after all whether it was worth his while to discourse so profoundly against an all-pervasive social tendency. It sure looks like a case of being so straight theoretically as to fall over backwards practically.
