Weary of working under rotten conditions for twelve long hours a day for the great sum of $3.50 and board, the drivers made up their minds to act in a concerted manner to straighten matters out. Every lumber camp in eastern Washington, northern Idaho and Montana has been seething with discontent for the last few months but the "jacks" have been curbing their impatience in an effort to work out a mature plan of action and to devise ways and means to insure the success of a strike that would get the goods. The strike has been anticipated for several months by the bosses and commented upon widely by the Capitalist press. Realizing that the winter cut of lumber must be taken out to. the mills during the comparatively short period of high water.he-me ber slaves selected this as the only logical time to strike. Great pains were taken to see to it that none of the disadvantages of a hasty or un organized strike would confront them. First of all the organisation and strike campaign were perfected, the date set and the demands worked out in detall. The result has been a complete tie-up in the Fontine region and the rapid spread of the strike to Eureka, Stillwater and other points. The drivers or "River Hogs." as the bosses call them, were the first to go out on strike. These men left the job in a bunch from Fontine and Eureka, kept themselves sober, camped out at Robert's spur, about a mile west of Fontine, sent out an appeal for solldarity and support and settled down in dead earnest to do their share in winning the strike. Gunmen were rushed to the scene at once, but no scabs were brought in. The private guards have not as yet started anything. The picket line is adequate and no trouble is anticipated. All the men on strike are more determined than ever to push the strike to a vic torious conclusion. INTERNATIONAL LABOR DAY May Day. 1917. finds the working class still waling in pools of its own blood-still fighting or preparing to fight the hopeless wars of the Masters of Bread. But, somehow, this year it is different, The great bleeding, blinded, long suffering giant of Labor is doing something he has never done before; he is, beginning to QUESTION. the justice of the implacable powers that forced him into such hellish byways of life. He is questioning the need of so much slaughter för so little gain, questioning the right of Autocracy to rule and of Parasitism to exist, questioning the high egst of living and the low value of life, questioning lies, hypocricy and sham-questioning Capitalism. And not only the men peering out over the muddy trenches at the so-called enemy, but men and women in the cities and on the farms of every land are wondering whether the misery and uncertainty of the past free years and of the present are really necessary, neces: sary in a fair, greett world-a May Day world--in which every twig and stem and blade of grass is budding, flowering and bursting forth with the irresistible life-sap of Springtime. In spite of the rigid censorship that is trying pretty successfully to keep the workers of different countries from finding out things worth knowing, enough truth has leaked through to show that the ideal of May Day is not dead and that working class solidarity is still very much alive and making the master class tremble with a fear that they have never felt before. The workers of Russia, for instance, faced the new bourgeois government with demands for the cessation of hostilities, a FOUR HOUR work day, 700 per cent increase in pay (the equivalent of confiscation), and holidays enough to make the head of any boss swim with dizzy horror. A. are not ALL. fools by any means.
