ALEXANDER ALDAMAS Striker Who Defended His Life From Murderous Thug Must be Saved from being Railroaded to Prison. The struggle of labor to improve its condition and to secure more huínan treatment is a constant warfare against capital. In this warfare the employers resort to every means in their power to down organized labor, destroy the unions of the workers and keep the wage slaves in subjection. One of the most brutal and despotic cases of this character was the fight of the Steamship Trust against the Marine Firemen's Union, in connection with the recent strike of the Transport Workers. The steamship companies were determined to break the strike and subdue the workers. With the aid of the police and of a horde of hired thugs they terrorized the strikers, clubbed and shot down the pickets, menaced with violence every active member of the Union. It was as the direct result of this policy of terror on the part of the masters that the striker Alexander Aldamas, a member of the Marine Firemen's Union, was forced to defend his life against an attacking party of the company's hirelings. On July 9, 1912, Aldamas was on picket duty for the Marine Firemen's Union. On the morning of that day a policeman, thout any provocation whater, shot and killed th picket Andreas Rodriguez, while another striker was shot in the back by a scab, the police refusing to aid the wounded worker or to arrest anyone who as saulted the pickets. The homes of the strikers and their union headquarters were repeat-dly invaded by police and scat and the strikes brutally assaulted Alexander Aldamas was doing picket duty when be was approached by one Gonzalez, who boasted of being a scab and supplying the steamship company with strikebreakers. He abused Aldamas and villifie the Union, finally challenging Al damas to fight. Aldamas refused to have any trouble with the scab and sought to avoid an encounter with him. But Gonzalez persisted, following the picket from place to place. Then Gonzalex, the AGENT PROVOCATEUR of the steamship company, gathered a gang of thugs and, drawing a stiletto, attacked Aldamas. The latter, fearin he would be killed like his fellow picket a few hours before, shot in self-defense, wounding Gonzalez in the leg. The trouble attracted a large crowd of scabs and strikers, and a number of shots were exchanged, as a result of which two policemen were wounded. Aldamas was arrested, and the steamship companies are determined to wreak their vengeance upon him. They propose to make an example of him, in order to terrorize his fellow workers into complete submission. With this aim in view, the authorities-with the Steamship Trust behind them have multiplied the charges against Aldamas out of the original accusation against him, that of wounding scab Gonzalez, the police have manufactured SEVEN separate indictments, charging him with the shooting of the policemen wounded in the affray, and also with corrying concealed weapons. The purpose of REJOICING OVER VICTORY. Solidarity: Detroit, Mich., Nov. 30. these proceedings is to pile up so many indictments against Aldamas that he can be sent to prison for 50 years. There is also the further purpose of the prosecution, even more brutally refined according to the laws of the State of New York, Alamas can be sent to prison FOR LIFE, as an habitual criminal, if convicted on ali the charges manufactured against him. Fellow workers! We appeal to every justice-loving man and woman not to permit this contemplated outrage. Will you stand quietly by, while Alexander Aldamas is railroaded to the penitentiary-perhaps for life because he was devoted to the interests of his union and because he de fended his life against an attacking party of scabs and thugs hired by the masters? Nor is it merely a question of Aldamas. It is not the MAN Aldamas that the bosses and the police want to punish. STRIKER Aldamas, the active and loyal union man, that is to be made an example of. In the person of Aldamas the Steam ship Trust seeks to give a blow to the whole labor movement, and to every workingman who struggles to improve conditions and who realizes that the bosses are explotters and parasites living upon his sweat and blood. Men and Women In the name of the cause of labor; in the name of humanity and the emancipation of the workers from wage slavery, we appeal to you to show your solidarity with Alexander Aldamas. He fought for the cause of labor, for YOUR cause, and it is THEREFORE that he is to be made a victim. We appeal to you-nay, we demand of you that you raise your voice in protest against the plutocratic conspiracy to railroad Aldamas to prison, against this contemplated outrage upon all labor; that you show to the masters that you cannot be intimidated and that you are alive to your true interests. BASIS OF FREE SPEECH Men whose ideals are destroyed, hampered or combatted are likely to want to destroy thing as they are. Every sincere and deep rela mer, whether in art, polities, religion or industry has ideals which he warmly cherishes, and if these ideals can not be made to apply to conditions, if they are combatted and suppressed, then the men and women holding them are likely to become "dangerous to society, to become revolutionary and destructive. Hence the great wisdom of toleration. If all sincere ideals were received in the hospitable social center of the public, if our newspapers, if our clubs, if our gatherings, if the community in general would welcome all ideas, no matter how subversive of present conditions, welcome them and consider them, not legislate against them, the destructive impulse which every thwarted idealist has would not be aroused. We would derive the good there is in every sincere idealist; it would serve to modify our prejudices and our injustice and our routine habits and at the same time take from what most of us dislike and fear, namely, violent revolutionary events. In this lies the immense importance of free speech and free expression in art, politics, and industrial matters. The people, the stand-patters, who, for profit or for an unthinking but sincere conservatism, try to suppress radical tendencies because they fear revolution and destruction, are themselves fostering revolution and destruction. The force and power of their reactionary intolerance tend to make the idealist desperate and induce him to resort to violence. He, therefore, who stands in the way of free speech and free expression in art, politics or industry is an enemy of society.- Hutebins Hapgood, in New York Globe. The above from the pen of one of the best known staff writers in the country, is being circulated in booklet form by the Free Speech League, with headquarters in New York City. It is re-enforced by similar opinions quoted from statements of doctors, professors and other intellectuals from the professional class. No doubt it is thoroughly worth while as propaganda anang the middle and upper classes, and will have effect on some of them towards disarming their opposition to the expression of revolutionary ideas. But it is poog dope to hand out to the working class, f asmuch as it tends to create the idea that free speech is dependent upon the will of All history opposes that conclusion. Its testimony all through the ages is eloquent with the fact that freedom of speech for any part of the subject class has always depended upon the degree of power and fighting spirit displayed by that element. "Free speech" can never be considered as a permanent and universal conquest so long as there remains ruling class. Traditions and constitutional provisions regarding freedom of speech and assemblage are of great value as "toe-hold" in a scrap; but the scrapping must not be lost sight of. The United States constitution "guarantees" tree speech; but that does not prevent the chief of police in the vil lage of Little Falls, N. Y., from putting In jail on account of it the mayor of the neighboring city of Schenectady, along with a strike committee and all other disturbers of the masters' peace. Time and again in Western cities, the l. W. W. bas bad to re-conquer the right to speak, by filling up jails and piling up the expense upon the taxpavers. That method seems to have deterred many another city from imitating the repressive tactics of their sisters. In no age, including our own, has the master class been imbued with humanitarian motives or a clear understanding of how to avoid disorder. Accustomed to rule, the master class DEMANDS obedience from its subjects. The ruling class organizes those subjects, not with any idea of the latter's interests, hopes and aspirations, but solely with the object to promote the masters' material interests. Anything that interferes with the material. interest of the masters is treason, and FORCE is invariably the masters' answer In its broadest sense, it is fortunate that such has been and is the case. Otherwise, bumanity might have halted in the pathway towards economic freedom, to repose for awhile on the rosy bed of a "benevolent slavery. FREE! BY THE MIGHT OF LABOR With Ettor, Giovannitts and Caruso free, we may now proceed to give our thanks and impressions to the world. When the history of this celebrated case is written it will be plainly seen that a new political and legal power has-arisen in this country, based on the industrial solidarity of labor, especially that of the textile workers. For the first time in the history of labor have governors' secretaries and capitalists taken the stand to testify in favor of labor leaders engaged in conflict with big corporations with which they are identified. Many were the acts of labor which brought about these results. It is reported that the Wood woolen mill at Lawrence, the greatest and best equipped in the world, had decreased 12 per cent in efficiency as a result of a systematic sabotage practiced in retaliation for the unjust imprisonment and trial of the three men. This is one of many salient acts, inflicting millions in loss, that made the capitalists anxious to sue for peace and grant labor victory. There are other acts, such as the exposure of dynamite plants and other infanies, whose continued publicity was productive of a huge anti-capitalist sentiment, which contributed to success. Back of the solidified textile workers in the mills at Lawrence stood the solidified workers of all lands. To those who took part in this memorable defense it will ever be a source of grateful recollection to think of the support that came from across the sess The workers of the industrial nations of Europe seemed to vie with each other in their acts and pledges of support. From France, Germany, England and Scotland, even from far-away Australia, came denunciatory resolutions, newspapers containing reports of meetings, money or ders and letters of sympathy and support. Cuba, from the Hawaiian Islands, from Canada, came the messages of good cheer and substantial aid. It was great; it was grand; the beginning of the world-wide brotherhood of toil in the shop that is destined to overthrow the international capitalist system of profit. Fifteen hundred men here are on strike against a cut in wages and the smaller properties are shut down dead. The Dome and Hollinger are the only two mines operating underground and neither of them have any experienced miners on their pav roll. The operators are moving heaven and earth to get strikebreakers in here, but there is practically nothing doing in that Only 15 have got through from the outside since the start of the strike, and everything in the garden is lovely. line About 150 gunmen and Thiel detectives are in camp to date and more coming on every train; they are pulling off a few raw stunts, when they get the boys alone But when we turn out in massed picket there is very strictly nothing doing. The Dome (Standard Oil) has got live wires strung around the property and we shrewdly surmise that they are kept quite as busy keeping men in there as they are keeping us out. Try and give us a good notice in your paper, as there are no strikebreakers to be had in this district and our only danger lies in the outside men coming in who know nothing about it. The good old government is doing its duty to the bunch who own it and the government railroad is making no bones about bringing men in here, all of which helps to educate the slaves of the district. Two gunmen are up for trial next Friday for pulling guns and trying to drive one of the Italian boys to work. Frank Radcliffe was arrested two days ago for ASKING QUESTIONS and was discharged next day, and another bunch of union men are to come up Friday for walking on the road, telling the truth about a scab, and counting the buckets coming up at the Hollinger, etc. It is evident that coercion of some kind is being used, as two strikebreakers, one at the Dome and one at the Hollinger, have gone crazy while at their work. And in fact there is no other reason for the gunmen's presence in the camp. Will send further details next week. MEMBER W. F. The methods used by some craft unions are well illustrated by an incident that happened in New Castle about two weeks ago. The Thompson-Starrett Construction Co. of Pittsburg is erecting a large building in this city and employs three or four stationary engineers. Albert Brest was running the engine on a big cement mixer. He was discharged by the boss, who admitted that he had done so on complaint of the other engineers who belonged to the Pittsburg local of Stationary Engineers' Union. No attempt was made by these "union" engineers to get Brest to join their organization. Yet we are told that the craft unions intend to organize all the workers!
