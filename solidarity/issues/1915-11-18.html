Some of the slaves at the Pumpelly Battery works have gone on strike, or rather quit. This compans employs about 150 men. The molding department, in which about 20 men work, is the only department affected. These men have been receiving 40 cents per hundred on piere work. One of them showed me his time slip, and he averaged about $4.00 per day. The company evidently thought this was too much. But instead of reducing the rate, they changed over to the premium system. Under this s'stem, the men are guaranteed $2.50 and 25 cents a hundred for all over a thousand pieces. This is equivalent to a reduction of 15 cents a hundred. So the slaves quit, and the company has filled their places. Suppose the men had staved on the job, and just turned out enough work to make $2.50 a dav, at the rate of 40 cents a hundred. The company would soon have had to raise the rate of pav or employ more men, in order to get the work out. But such ideas never penetrate the skull of our free American workingman. He would rather assert his independence, by quitting a "ad Joss," and then tramp the streets in search of a good one. If these slaves live long enough, they will find that all bosses are bad, and will be until the slaves organize and make them good. J. W. BARTON.
