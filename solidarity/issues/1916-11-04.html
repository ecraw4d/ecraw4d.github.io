(Special to Solidarity) Philadelphia, Pa. The Loomfixers' Union with 500 members in Philadelphia, recently declared a strike to enforce their demands for more wages. It was on Oct. 18th that they struck after trying to come to an agreement with the textile barons, The loomfixers have succeeded in breaking the ranks of the barons. The loom fixer is the aristocrat of the textile industry, so aristocratic that he thinks he is a part of the firm and acts accordingly. That is, he has been acting that way until the firm refused to grant him $8 more per week than he was getting prior to Oct. 18 $22 per week. Now he acts differently, because he senses the doom of his aristocracy, despite the fact that about 190 fixers returned to work under the new wage scale, $25 per week. This increase was secured in small shops, but they will repudidate it unless the big manufacturers grant it also, which is unlikely. This increase is infinitesimal when you take into consideration the fact that there are very few fixers employed in a shop about one fixer to every 50 textile workers. The Loomfixers' Union is not affiliated with the A. F. of L It is independent. About 5,000 textile workers are affected by the strike. The weavers who are thrown out of work as a result of the strike, held a meeting Oct. 23rd under the auspices of the A. F. of L. Cloth Weavers' Union, Local 72, which has a membership close to 1,000. The meeting was held in the Light House, Lehigh Ave. and Masher St. The sentiment was against the fixers, made so by the officials of the A. F. of L., because the Loomfixers' won't affiliate with that organization. It was voted at the meeting that un less the fixers affiliate with the A. F. of L., they, the Weavers, would return to work and take back with them A. F. of L. loomfixers. If there should not be enough fixers to keep the looma in operation, they would fix their own looms.
