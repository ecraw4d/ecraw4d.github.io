THE “CHANGING” DEBS The West Virginia situation has not only helped to emphasize the well-known brutality of the capitalist class and its official tools of the political state; but has also furnished a glaring instance of the stupid incapacity if not the small-minded crookedness of some socialist politicians. Our readers will remember that the socialist party recently sent a committee of three prominent members to West Virginia to investigate the situation and report on same to the membership. The committee consisted of the "peerless" 'Gene Debs, Adolph Germer, and Victor L. Berger. Their report has just been published in various socialist papers. The three or four columns of fine print contain no record of events in the West Virginia mine war; but consist almost entirely of a very carefully detailed attempt to prove Governor Hatfield innocent of the numerous charges made against him by the West Virginia socialists themselves, and for which all the socialist papers of that state were suppressed, their editors nearly all put in jail, and the printing plant of the Huntington "Socialist and Labor Star" taken possession of and destroyed by militiamen. Commenting on Debs' actions and the report of the socialist committee, the "Socialist and Labor Star" of June 13 says in part: "The current expression among the socialists in and around Charleston in commenting on Debs' action and words was, 'Debs has changed; Debs has been tricked, deceived as to the true conditions of affairs. From a perusal of the committee's report you would naturally gather the impression that the committee was sent here for no other purpose than to clear Hatfield of the charges the West Virginia socialists have made against him. It abounds with the same sickening laudatory comments on Hatfield that have filled the servile daily press of the state for months past couched in some instances in the same identical language. For instance, "The governor assured us that he was not opposed to the pending senatorial investigation." The committee seems to have taken undue pains to deny things for Hatfield that he has never denied himself. Take for instance the threat to deport the miners if they did not accept, his proposal and go to work. The committee says he never made such threat. We say that it appeared in the Charleston dailies, during the time the miners' convention was being held. It was quoted and attributed to the governor as his own words. And he has never publicly denied it. The committee further heralds forth the information that the mine guard system in West Virginia has been abolished by act of the legislature. With just a little investigating upon their part they could have found that this was untrue. The only thing the legislature did was to forbid sheriff's from appointing mine guards as deputies. Take this gem from the report of supposedly free men on a lawful mission: "The governor informed us that we were AT LIBERTY TO GO ANYWHERE WE PLEASED in pursuit of our mission."
