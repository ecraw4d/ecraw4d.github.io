He is often an realist, whose ideals are the reaction from, and the result of, his environ ment. "World trade" is the cause of world wars. "Patriotism," "Anti-Prussianism" and "the Rights of Small Nations," are but devices behind which to hide the fact. The personnel of the labor movement ever changes. But it goes right on, without any alteration in its principle of class war. The labor movement often wins and more often loses. But win or lose, it cannot be exterminated, for it is the creature of forces more powerful than either itself or its conquerors; and is driven on by them to strive for the overthrow of the latter. The right "to hire and to fire" employees without union interference, is claimed by New York's boss cloakmakers. The big strike now on proves the right to be more easily asserted than enforced. And by the same token, it looks as if the day when labor will exert its power "to Fire" the boss, grows nearer. Cooper in his book, "The Fate of The Middle Classes," quotes a German economist, Ernest Von Halle, as follows: "Modern production by means of steam-driven machinery cannot stand unlimited competition, which too often leads to the destruction of the value of large capitals. Machine production requires close technical regulation and does not admit of economic anarchy." SOLIDARITY, CLEVELAND; OHIO the control of the Money Trust. Its enactments have not destroyed the power of that trust, but have made possible the great war financial operations in which it took the leading, and most profitable part. His trust measures have come to nought. The idea of dissolving trusts into competitive constituents is proven à failure by the hearings against the Standard Oil Co., now being conducted before the industrial trade commission. And the attempt to block interlocking combinations has given way to the necessities for such combinations in the development of foreign trade. The war has prevented and upset whatever tariff ideas he may have desired to put into force. It has turned him into a proponent of many of the measures of big capital, which he was pledged to, and did fight as in the Mexican and preparedness issues. First opposed to Mexican invasion he is now opposed to Amèr ican withdrawal from that unfortunate country and his preparedness is in larger proportions than that ever planned by any of the representatives of big capital who preceeded him in office. Wilson represents middle class weakness in the struggle with big capital and the economic development which it compels both at home and abroad; abroad because at home, and at home because abroad. Wilson's one great act was to keep the country out of the European war. This act, while of great human benefit to this country, was also of great financial benefit to its dominant interests, and to their friends, the Allies with whom they are united by common capitalist interests. His international policy was, in actual practice and results, both pro-big capitalist and pro-Ally, which are one and the same thing. The capitalists have nothing to fear in Wilson. But they don't want him as the times needs more aggression and oppression if capitalism is to develop to its fullest and ripest. Capitalism is forced to look for more markets in a manner devoid of vacillation. It requires decisive, single-purposed action. It requires a governmental head who will never pull back the capitalist horse by the tail, but will keep the road clear of all obstacles, so that it may plunge along at top speed. That's what a good governmental head is for. Wilson, don't fill the bill, and, so will not do. for big capital. One thing is certain: Labor cannot get improvement and emancipation from any capitalist candidate so long as capitalism endures and industrial unionism is not an established fact.
