W. proper, his application would be referred to the local union of his industry, already in existence; or to the district organizer with a view to getting an organization started in that industry. League members, as such, had no voice or vote in the administrative affairs industrial unions. But members of industrial unions, with inclination or abilities as propagandists, could join the League and take part in its activities. In this way, all the active propagandists in a given industrial center would be brought together, and the general and specific educational needs of the district would be provided for.
