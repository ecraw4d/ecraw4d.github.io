NEW RAILROAD WORKERS personally know of two men who have made each over $100 in STICKERS FOR LOCAL No. 600 one week, working overtime of course. These were riveters, One of them made in six weeks enough to buy a Ford automobile. Of course, when such a fat paycheck is cut off in its prime, the beneficiary of it is bound to feel wroth. I have talked with some of these high-priced riveters, and they have little or no sympathy for the strikers. If the heater boys allow themselves to back to work, and placed under the jurisdiction of the boilermakers, I foresee bad times looming ahead for them. They seem to instinctively feel this, and I think they, or many of them, would rather go back unorganized than in such an organization. coaxed Some of the boys, not over 17 or 18 years old, already look like old men. But there is no doubt, that if they were actively supported by the unions already intrenched in the works that they could win hands down. In fact there would be nothing to it. There is a big demand for skilled metal workers of all descriptions and there are several large ships in the yards nearly ready to be launched, with contracts ahead for the full capacity of the plant for years to come. Every delay means thousands of dollars for the corporation, which by the way, is owned by our friend Schwab of Bethlehem and other places. He has installed one of the finest speeding systems here that can be found in our "glorions" country, and I have heard it stated, with what truth I know not, that an ordinary freighter of 5,000 or 6,000 tons capacity could be assembled and put into commission from one to three months earlier than in any other shipyard in the country. The strike is only two days old, and of course it is foolish to venture into any prophecy as to the outcome, but if the sentiment I saw displaved is any indication, and the A. F. of L. does not succeed in smothering the spirit of revolt, I think that most of the demands will be conceded. But San Francisco is the hardest town in the United States for the I W W to sécure a foothold in. On the job, I mean.
