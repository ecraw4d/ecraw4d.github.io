Unrest Epidemic SOLIDARITY, CLEVELAND, OHIO The results of all these strikes prove that an open field exists for the organization that will manage to triumph over this difficult situation, Such a triumph is only likely to come after oft-repeated and futile attempts, productive of greater class experience, knowledge, ability and action. Just now the migratory workers are achieving great results In the harvest fields. They are rolling up. I. W. W. members by the thousands and developing organizers of ability. These migratory workers will drift back to the cities in a few more weeks. It is hoped that the zeal and enthusiasm in behalf of the I. W. W. which characterises their activities in the summer will not be permitted to go to sleep in the fall, winter and spring. The migratory workers should not drift back to the cities only to hibernate there. They should conquer John Trust Capitalist as they have conquered John Small Farmer. The assistance they have given to the iron ore strikers the impetus and cash that they have given to Minnesota organization and relief-should find new outlets in the big cities. They should help to build up job organization and secure job control in them as well as in the hay and wheat fields. They should join the industrial union of the industry in which they get a job and induce others on the job to do likewise. If they get jobs in machine shops, let them transfer into and help build up the metal and machinery industrial union. If they work as building laborers, or concrete workers, let them join the building construction industrial union. If they are omnibusses, dishwashers, porters, rangemen, waiters, etc., the hotel and restaurant workers' union is the place for them. So on all down the line. Le them get into and build up the union of the industry at which they may happen to be employed. Where no organization exists let one be started. The idea of an I. W. W. member sleeping on the ground where his activities are most necessary should be abandoned. A few thousand I. W. W.'s going into the industries of the middle west and east without any brass bands or noise to recruit real industrial unionists is a suggestion that should be received with active approval. The I. W. W. must prove itself competent to retain and build up its membership in accordance with the varying conditions of capitalism, no matter what the latter's stage of development may be. The I. W. W. has certainly got to rise triumphantly over the great trusts that abound in the middle west and east, or else admit The strategic battlefield is where the trusts loom large; not its futility. where the small farmers are most abundant.
