Seattle, Wash., April 30. The defense in case of Tracy concluded its case on Saturday. This morning prosecution started rebuttal. Defense concluded with testimony of Tracy himself and that of Harry Golden, the boy who lost his leg on "Verona." Tracy denied all allegations of conspiracy and gun toting. Came out brilliantly. The general feeling is that the defense has covered the ground thoroughly as possible and as had remarkable series of witnesses, including several deputy sheriffs who have proved perjury of ex-Sheriff McRae. Today (Monday) prosecution introduced several witnesses of trivial nature. Also the Judas traitor ex-I. W. W. Charles Auspos, one of defendants who turned state's evidence. Auspos swore that man in cell with him said he fired shot, also repeated other conversations alleged to have taken place between him and others on boat. Cross-examined by defense coun sel, he admitted that although charged with murder he was not in prison, but out at Port Orchard, a nearby bay resort. This discredited his evidence a good deal. Next witness was the Pinkerton George Reese, member I. W. W., member Longshoremen's Union and delegate for Longshoremen on Seattle Central Labor Council. Swore to seeing guns on boat and swore to remarks by members regarding possession of guns. Was confronted with sworn affidavit made in presence of notary public and taken stenographically in presence of Fred Moore, couple of months previously. In this he swore no guns, no threats or talk of guns. Thus in open court the traitor squirmed when confronted by proofs of his perjury. That any jury could believe statements by such a snake and obvious seller to highest bidder is unbelievable. Case will go to jury in few days. By end of week we shall see the case go to the Grand Jury. Entire working class awaits the result of this trial. Seattle, Wash., April 26. The end is now in sight of the case of Thomas H. Tracy, the workingman charged with murder by the Lumber Trust of the Northwest. The result of his case will soon be known to the workers of America and the world; and from that result we may judge the probable fate of the other 72 workingmen awaiting trial on a similar charge. The effects of Everett's Bloody Sunday have been far sweeping. This case and the Mooney trial have both served to awaken the workers to the ruthlessness with which the masters will try to crush organization and stifle Free Speech. Ex-Citizen Deputy Testifies. Last Saturday the stand was taken by Jóseph Schofield, who was a witness for the defense. Schofield was working in a shingle mill in Everett at the time of the tragedy and was practically forced by his employers into joining the so-called citizen deputies. On the night of October 30, when the deputies were rounding up some workingmen who had come to Everett to hold a meeting, in the darkness Schofield was mistaken for one of the Free Speech advocates and was badly beaten up by his own associates. He He received from "Governor" Clough, prominent mill-owner and open shop suppórter of Everett, $75 to compensate him for his injuries. He Stated that Sheriff McRae and the deputy who assailed him were both drunk that night. Ex-Sheriff's Testimony Further Impeached. He backed up the statements of Fred Luke, McRae's right-hand man, regarding the presence of the Sheriff at the brutal beating and gauntlet-running at Beverly on October 30, a charge which the sheriff had given a sworn denial. The time has come when we must either organize or perish. Industrial conscription would force us to be slaves; military conscription murderers. The emancipation of the working class is impossible without the abolition of the wage-system. Industrial freedom means the complete negation of exploitation. In order to be free, the workers must put a stop to social parasitism-the exploitation of man by his fellow-man for all time. The I. W. W. is organized and is fighting for this very purpose. Kick in. A labor organization stands either for the working class or against it. There is no middle ground. Either it is an enemy of Capitalism a of either loyal to the workers or traitor to them. And anything less than complete loyalty is reason. Men like Sam Gompers, Viviani and Walling will go down in history as the Judases and Arnolds of the Labor movement. The well-fed labor-skates who turn the workers over to the boss in times of peace with contracts and agreements would logically turn them over to the murderous butchers of Wall Street in time of war. It's perfectly natural, and it has happened just that If the great American Scissorbill would wash his face and clean his finger-nails he'd lose the only part of "his country" he ever had to fight for. Only by organizing into One Big Union can the workers prove themselves strong enough to DEMAND the full product of their labor. Only by fighting unceasingly against the hideous System of Exploitation can they prove themselves WORTHY of having the full product of their labor. Freedom of the press and of speech and of assembly are far more worth fighting for than the fabled "freedom of the seas." The U-boats of the Kaiser aren't half as dangerous to the workers of America as are the clubs, guns and slugshots of the private armies of the master class at home. The place to fight for your country is on the job. Capitalism, blinded with lust, drunken with power and swollen with the bloody increase of its own crookedness, is staggering to its doom to the tune of patriotic airs. It is stumbling to its downfall, deafened to the wails of the millions beneath its feet by the plaudits of whores, the panegyrics of idiots and the roar of sixteen-inch guns. But the I. W. W., despised, ridiculed and persecuted, is patiently, stubbornly, ceaselessly "building the structure of the new society within the shell of the old." Pending before the state legislature is a bill which makes it a serious crime, punishable by heavy fine and imprisonment, to rent halls or buildings to any organization which advocates sabotage. The bill is, of course, aimed at the L W. W., and doubtess a very large percentage indeed of the people of this state would be glad to have it passed, if such action would stamp out the I. W. W. But if this bill is passed, it will have the effect of making martyrs out of the I. W. W. Sabotage, which many leaders of the I. W. W. have repeatedly stated is undoubtedly but a passing phase of the industrial union movement, would become enthroned as a sacred principle if such a bill were passed. There's nothing like repression to make men and organizations fight like wildcats. The history of Russia proves that.
