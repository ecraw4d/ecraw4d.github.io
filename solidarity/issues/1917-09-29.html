THE CLASS STRUGGLE AND THE STRUGGLE FOR LIFE The history of the human race is largely the story of how the biped known as man has acquired his food, clothing and shelter. Every living creature has been compelled to either find sustenance for itself or perish. The evolution of society from the primitive gens, clan and phratery of prehistoric times to the complicated governmentalism of today, has been brought about by the efforts of the different parts of the human family to organize or group themselves together in order to successfully combat conditions which inhibit their chances to survive. As long as man was battling with rugged Nature, and his four-footed enemies, the struggle was primarily that of the human animal, per se, against the hostile environment of the younger world. But with the advent of the institution of human slavery, when society became differentiated into opposing classes of masters and slaves, the struggle has not only been between man and nature but between man and man-between class and class. In the beginning the primitive human being went forth each day with his stone ax or bow and arrows to find the necessary sustenance for himself and those nearest to him. Just as life developed from unicellular to multicellular in the depths of the sea, before the advelt of man on the globe, so human society developed from the simple to the complexfrom the weak to the powerful in order that human beings might triumph over the forces arrayed against them in the struggle for life. All forms of organization and all weapons used by man in the The means used by man to reach his present state were dictated by sternest necessity. He had to use the weapons and social groupings that would give him the greatest advantage over his enemies, or he would lose all chances to survive. It is the same today, Now that the struggle of the human race to find its "place in the sun" has been lifted from the natural to the social plane, it is following the same general lines as it progreses. Just as the stone ax of palaeolithic days developed into the forty-two centimeter gun of today; just as the arrow evolved into the aeroplane and the clan into the Nation, so are all of the weapons and social groupings that have helped men conquer nature being readjusted to help him conquer dustry.
