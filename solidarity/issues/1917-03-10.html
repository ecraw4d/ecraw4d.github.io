Behind steel bars, with serious charges hanging over each one of them, and with a ghastly network of perjured, purchased testimony drawing ever closer about them, seventy-four members of the I. W. W. are going on trial for their lives and liberty in the city of Seattle, Washington. Witnesses are being bribed or bullied to testify against them. "Gumshoe" artists are scouring the country, leaving no stone unturned to secure any "evidence," by any means, that will prejudice the opinions of jurymen or poison the mind of the public against them. The Lumber Trust and the labor-hating henchmen of Big Business on the Pacific coast are prepared to go to any length in order to railroad or hang these men. It is an important part of their plan to wipe out unionism and establish the open shop generally on the Western coast that each of these union men be convicted and sentenced. This is the opening battle of their war against opposition to their bloodthirsty greed for more profits and power. Thomas Tracy, one of the rvivors of the bloody massacre of union men by the hired thugs and respectable members of the Everett Commercial Club, is the first to be brought before his accusers. Cleareyed and unfaltering, he goes into the jaws of the legal machinery of the court of Snohomish county, to come out either a victim of Capitalistic vengeance or as an example of how the working class of this country can protect its own. Five union men are dead and seventy-four in danger. Nothing on earth can save the men in jail in Seattle save the immediate and united action of the awakened working class of the land. You have seen other labor unions organized on craft, trade, lines. Craft unjonism, means that any small section of an industry has a labor union separate from all other sections that cannot acf in any concerted movement of labor because of this craft separation. For example, in the railroad industry there are the engineers' union, the fireman's union, the conductors' union, the brakeman's union, the switchmen's union and many others on that road and in the shops and yards. Each union acts for itself and usually has time agreements with the companies for a term of years, each agreement ending at a dif ferent time than the others. When one craft union goes on strike at the end of the time agreement the other craft unions keep at work and by remaining on the job act as scabs and strike-breakers in defeating their fellow workers of the craft on strike. Everstt, Washington, under which constitutional rights of citizens, namely, the right of peaceful assemblance and free speech, have been ignored, and citi zens have been shot down in attempts to exercise these fundamental rights, WHEREAS there are, at the present time, imprisoned in the State of Wash Ington many fellow-workers whose only offense against the peace and dignity of the State of Washington, seems to have been that because of their efforts at organization of the workers in the lumbering industry they had incurred the enmity of the Employers' Association, and WHEREAS we consider that a con dition of anarchy has arisen in Ev erett which is merely the result of an attempt of the timber and lumber barons and other corporate interests of the Pacific Coast to stifle in embryo any attempt at organization in this in dustry, and that if this method of dealing with the workers is permitted to prevail in any section of the country unrebuked, that sooner or later we may be confronted by similar con ditions ourselves, and WHEREAS we believe the action of these interests, subservient city and county officials at Everett, to have been un-American and worthy only of the contempt of every liberty-loving eitisen: THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Oregon State Federation of Labor, in convent assembled, hereby condemns the action of the civic authorities of Everett, Washington, by permitting these outrages to have tak en place, and we hereby pledge our moral support and recommend that af filiated organizations use proper ef fort to ald morally and financially in the effort to establish rights guaran teed under the constitution; AND IT BE FURTHER RESOLVED: That coples of this resolution be sent to the Governor of the State of Wash ington, and the President of the United States; and that we urge upon our representatives in Congress a full Federal investigation of the Everett tragedy.
