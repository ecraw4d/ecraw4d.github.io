"CONTEMPT OF GOVERNMENT" SOLIDARITY CONTINUE THE FIGHT AT HOME Solidarity has received the following communication for publication: Fellow Worker: Peoria, Illinois, Aug. 19, 1913. Last Spring the members of Local 27, Metal and Machinery Workers, I. W. W., of Peoria, called out on strike members work ing at the Avery Engine & Implement Co.'s plant, because some of them were being fired for trying to organize the place, which was and is an open shop. The strike met with the most determined opposition of the company and the city authorities, and the I. W. W. was obliged to temporarily withdraw. The strike was on from May 18 until June. This local union was organized only about four months at the time of the strike, which was also a reason for withdraw ing from the fight. Now the Avery Co. has just sent the police department a check for $100 by one of its representatives, who made a very complimentary speech to the force, thanking them for their faithfulness to duty and the flag; thus showing that the company has plenty of dough to give to the bulldogs of the masters, but none to increase the bread supply of the producer and his family. The Avery Company makes steam and gas tractor engines, threshers, hullers, corn plows, breaking plows, and a lot of other farm machinery, and as they are a scab concern every I. W. W. ought to refuse and induce others to refuse, to work with any of the Avery machinery. The farmer will kick them to the salesman, who will kick to his master, who will probably sit up and take notice. Let us make the Avery strike as wide as the territory in which their implements sell.
